+- **Teams over the hard limit get no pairs: a deliberate safety bound, not a fixed defect (autograder V2 check HADOOP-PAIRS-WRONG; documented in step E12; analysed in session 3, repair round R3, no code change, kept by human decision under instructor condition 4).** J07 rejects a team with more than `scholarchain.teams.hardLimit` authors (default 5,000; `modules/hadoop/src/main/resources/scholarchain-hadoop-defaults.xml:188-193`) with reason `team_exceeds_hard_limit`, so no team and no pairs are written. The rejection is counted and reported, as the specification asks ("count and report unusually large author teams", `docs/agent-runs/phase-17/course-specification.md:1155`): OVERSIZE_AUTHOR_TEAMS, the per-reason reject counter, and a record in `rejects/v1/`. Why it stays: pairs already stream in shards of 100,000, so the risk is output size, not memory. Without the limit only the 1,048,576-byte DBLP record limit would bound a team: about 52,000 authors at 20 bytes per `<author>` element (a 3-character name), or about 30,000 at about 35 bytes (assumed), giving 1,351,974,000 or 449,985,000 pairs from one record; the hard limit bounds one record to 12,497,500 pairs. At about 693 bytes per pair record, a local measurement with no committed record, that would be about 937 GB or 312 GB per pair stage, about 0.9 to 2.8 TB over the three written stages, or about 1.25 to 3.75 TB with the shuffle; these volumes are estimates. The specification lists decompression-bomb inputs among the threats (`:2718`) and requires bounded inputs and outputs (`:2729`). No team reached the limit in Phase 15 experiment A, where the largest team had 137 authors. Not built: option A, which would replace materialized pairs with an implicit clique handled in Spark and avoids the volume only if Spark never expands it. A contract test added in R3 pins the bound (`JobLevelTeamScaleSuite`, "B3 safety bound"). Details: [`docs/hadoop-job-contracts.md`](../docs/hadoop-job-contracts.md#132-teams-over-the-hard-limit-are-rejected-check-hadoop-pairs-wrong).
