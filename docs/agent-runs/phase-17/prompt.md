### 6. Step 1: rag-engineer (L2's open findings, fail closed)

Read `docs/agent-runs/phase-16/review-report-P16-L-round2.md` (P16-L-10 to L-15, with their failure inputs), `repair-E01-rag.md` (the current fail-closed rule), `ClaimVerification.scala` and its tests, and the services golden-example suite and the fake benchmark (they are direct users of the verifier).

1. **One rule, fail closed (tier 1).** Change only how claims about the absence of a connection are recognised and checked, so that each of P16-L-10 to L-15 is caught by a rule that does not depend on listing every phrasing:
   a. **Absence recognition (P16-L-10), by predicate and negation scope, not by any negation token:** for a claim that names both researchers of a pair, the verifier looks for (i) a **connection predicate** from a fixed list (at least: connected, connects, path, linked, reachable, collaborated, coauthored, coauthors, same component) under a negation from a fixed list (at least: no, not, never, none, without, cannot, neither, nor, and every contraction ending in n't, such as aren't, isn't, haven't, hasn't, didn't, don't, can't, won't, after normalizing curly apostrophes) that governs it ("no path connects A and B", "A and B never collaborated"), or (ii) an **absence predicate** from a fixed list (at least: disconnected, unconnected, unreachable, isolated, separate, separated, apart, different component, another component) that is **not** itself negated. Either one makes the claim an absence claim. A negated absence predicate ("A and B are not disconnected") is a connection claim and is checked as connection claims are today. **When the scope is unclear** (a negation that may govern either predicate, or both kinds of predicate in one claim, for example "there is no evidence that A and B are disconnected"), the claim is treated as ambiguous and can be at most `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`, never `VERIFIED` (fail closed). The lists and the scope rule live in one place in the code, with a test per list entry class. This is a small, tested rule, not a language parser.
   b. **Third names (P16-L-11):** a word in an absence claim counts as a possible third name, and blocks `VERIFIED`, when it is (i) capitalised (the first word of a sentence included) and not part of either named researcher's name and not on a fixed allowlist (for example sentence starters such as The, There, No; month names; the metric and status words the verifier already knows), or (ii) equal, ignoring case, to any part of the name of any researcher in the evidence bundle other than the pair, unless that word is also part of one of the pair's own names ("John" in a claim about John Smith is not a third name just because a John White is in the bundle). One-word and lowercase names count.
   c. **Found paths anywhere (P16-L-12):** a cited or bundled found path that contains both researchers anywhere on it (endpoints or inner nodes) contradicts an absence claim.
   d. **Time qualifiers (P16-L-13):** any year or year range in an absence claim that differs from the searched window, or any year used with before, after, since, until, prior or between, blocks `VERIFIED`.
   g. **Closed vocabulary for absence claims:** every word of an absence claim must come from a fixed set: the pair's name parts; the predicate and negation lists of item a; a fixed list of function and scope words (for example of, in, within, between, up, to, hops, path, the, and, any, coauthorship, window); numbers, years and year ranges; and item b's allowlist. **Any other word** (an unknown lowercase name such as "carlos", a new participant, unlisted relationship wording) makes the claim ambiguous: never `VERIFIED`. rag-engineer builds the function-word list from the correct absence claims in the existing fixtures and shows by test that those claims stay eligible where their evidence supports them; a regression test covers an unknown lowercase participant with an otherwise complete pairwise `path=none` result.
   f. **Positive eligibility (the default):** an absence claim can be `VERIFIED` only when every one of these is established (item g included): it was recognised by (i) or (ii) of item a without ambiguity; both researchers are resolved; no possible third name (item b); a stated depth and window that match a cited, complete `path=none` search (items d, e); no found path between the pair (item c). A claim that names the pair, carries a negation word near them and matches no listed predicate is ambiguous (item a), never `VERIFIED`. Nothing becomes `VERIFIED` merely because no blocker matched. Regression tests also cover a sentence-initial third name, a lowercase third name, an unlisted relationship wording, and a contracted negation ("A and B haven't collaborated") with a cited found path between them, which must not be `VERIFIED`.
   e. **P16-L-14 and P16-L-15:** an absence claim with no stated depth is never `VERIFIED` against a depth-limited search, and an absence claim about unnamed people (for example "no common coauthors") is never `VERIFIED`; give each the status and reason code the finding's own text asks for, or, if it asks none, the weakest status the verifier already uses for an over-claim.
2. **Monotonic, with positive controls (tier 1):** every change may only lower a claim's status, never raise one. A test runs the existing claim fixtures (the verifier's own suites, the golden examples, the fake benchmark's claims) and shows that no claim's status rose; every status that fell is listed in the report with the clause of item 1 that lowered it. **Positive controls:** tests with affirmative connection claims that contain negation words (at least "A and B are not disconnected" and "A and B are not only connected but frequent coauthors", each with a cited found path) must keep the status they have today; and no existing affirmative connection claim in the fixtures may fall unless item 1's ambiguity clause names it. **No production limit, threshold, prompt or benchmark label is changed to make a test pass.** If a golden example's expected output changes, rag-engineer stops and reports it with the clause that changed it (the golden files are service-engineer's, "interface: yes"); the orchestrator then routes `repair-00-service`: service-engineer updates only the expected verification statuses that follow from item 1's rule, with the justification per changed line in its report, and code-reviewer checks each changed line in R-1.
